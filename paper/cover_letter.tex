\begin{letter}{The Editors-in-Chief\\ \textit{Quantum Information Processing}\\ Springer Nature}

\opening{Dear Editors,}

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, ``Budget-Aware Neural Error Mitigation for Variational Quantum Algorithms,'' by Guilin Zhang, Kai Zhao, and Xu Chu, for consideration as a Research Article in \textit{Quantum Information Processing}. The work concerns quantum error mitigation for variational quantum algorithms (VQAs) on near-term hardware.

The paper asks how mitigation methods compare at equal quantum cost. Zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE), Clifford data regression (CDR) and a trained neural model need very different numbers of circuit executions per mitigated estimate (3--5, 32 and 1), yet they are usually compared at a fixed number of shots per execution. We evaluate all three under a protocol in which every method spends the same total number of shots per evaluation point, and we introduce a neural error mitigation (NEM) model conditioned on the shot-noise scale so that one network serves all budgets.

Our three contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Budget-conditioned neural mitigation:} one network, one execution per evaluation, serving all shot budgets via $1/\sqrt{S}$ conditioning.
  \item \textbf{An equal-budget evaluation protocol}, with an explicit accounting of quantum and classical offline costs.
  \item \textbf{A characterization of when folding ZNE fails:} under coherent control-offset miscalibration it returns a worse answer than no mitigation on 38--63\% of instances, a rate that projecting its estimates onto the physical range does not change.
\end{itemize}

In simulation with matched training and test noise, NEM is the most accurate method at total budgets up to $2^{14}$ shots under miscalibration ($+72$--$81\%$) and the only method that beats unmitigated execution at those budgets under stochastic noise; CDR overtakes it at $2^{16}$ shots. Under structured noise, results hold from 4 to 20 qubits with a model retrained at each size. On an IBM Heron processor, folding ZNE is worse than no mitigation on up to 88\% of a small set of four-qubit instances, and a twin-trained model fine-tuned on 120 device-measured circuits reduces error by 39\% on held-out instances from the same calibration epoch.

We state the scope of these results carefully. The comparison with ZNE is structurally favourable to a trained model, which sees representative training data and device features; we claim no general superiority over ZNE and no formal error bound, CDR is more accurate wherever its cost is affordable, and the hardware results are a small-sample feasibility study on one device.

All simulation code and per-instance result data are public at \url{https://github.com/GuilinDev/Quantum-Error-Mitigation}, and the manuscript includes a complete Declarations section.

A related workshop version (arXiv:2609.38713) was an oral talk at the NeurIPS 2026 SaTQuML workshop, which is non-archival with no proceedings; it is cited in the manuscript. This manuscript is not under consideration at any other journal or archival venue, and the authors have no competing interests to declare. Thank you for your consideration.
